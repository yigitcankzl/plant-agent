# Technical Memorandum — GLWA 120-inch PCCP Transmission Main Break (Lake Huron WTP to Imlay City), August 13, 2022

**Case:** `glwa_120in_break`
**Facility:** Great Lakes Water Authority 120-inch PCCP transmission main break (Lake Huron WTP to Imlay City), Michigan, August 13, 2022
**Source:** Great Lakes Water Authority, Incident Report for 120-inch Water Transmission Main Break of August 13, 2022, December 20, 2022 (report to EGLE under Rule R325.12304), with appendices: PICA NFT inspection report and Xylem/Pure Technologies PipeWalker inspection reports (public record)
**Model:** `muse-spark-1.3-contributor`

## Purpose

Determine from incident timeline, operating data, pipe history and post-break electromagnetic (EM) inspections:

1. Probable cause of August 13, 2022 120-inch PCCP failure ~0.5-1 mi west of LHWTP — was operating pressure or surge involved?
2. Does rest of inspected main need immediate replacement?
3. What design, operation and emergency-response changes should prevent/limit repeat?

## Background

* Main: 120-inch diameter (p.7), approximately 26 miles from Lake Huron Water Treatment Plant (LHWTP) to Imlay City Booster Pump Station (IMC) (p.7).
* Pipe: prestressed concrete cylinder pipe (PCCP) — steel cylinder and concrete with reinforcing wire wrapped outside (p.7, p.126); embedded PCCP in this main (p.126); PICA reports constructed 1968 using embedded PCCP (p.126).
* History: installed early 1970s (p.7); in continuous service since mid-1970s with exception of one reported break in 1985 (p.9).
* Function: main is sole conduit to transmit finished water from LHWTP west to IMC (p.9). Normally 20 to 30 percent of overall GLWA system drinking water flows are provided by LHWTP (p.8). At break LHWTP was providing over 25 percent with estimated flow ~162 MGD (p.11).
* Downstream: potable water can be directed to 20-million-gallon reservoir and repumped during peak demand or bypassed during low demand (p.9). From IMC, flow can be pumped or bypassed to 72-inch main that runs west (p.9); system restored with flow LHWTP to IMC, and flow south through 96-inch main to NSC (p.15). NSC originally designed to pump from Detroit-based plants (primarily Northeast WTP) to Flint in 1970s, mode had not been used in more than 30 years (p.8).
* Break detected: Saturday, August 13, 2022 at approximately 4:30 a.m., sudden drop in pressure noted (p.10); experienced significant failure on August 13th, 2022 at 4:30 AM (p.126).
* Location: flooding approximately one-half mile due west of facility (p.11) vs failed approximately 1 mile west of treatment plant (p.126) / accessed approximately 1 mile from plant (p.128).
* Appearance: rupture occurred near invert and on south side of pipe (p.128); portion of inner cylinder located approximately 100 feet from break (p.11).
* Response: GLWA team on site within two hours (p.16); since 120-inch was feeding IMC reservoir during peak season, break isolated within five hours once LHWTP was offline (p.16); 108-inch butterfly valve closed at LHWTFP to allow service to Burtchville (p.16).
* Repair: 120-inch repair/closure piece with laying length 16-feet priority shipped from Texas arrived August 14 (p.13); order for three additional lengths placed with Thompson Pipe Company South Beloit, Illinois (p.13); two new 16-foot-long lengths made by Thompson arrived August 28 (p.18); total three 16-foot lengths replaced (p.15); each joint welded (p.18); inspected August 23-24 accessed where three (~16ft) segments removed (p.15, p.127).
* Return: repair completed Friday, September 2 (p.15); main returned to service Friday, September 30, all restrictions lifted (p.15, p.19); City of Flint restored normal service October 13, 2022 (p.19); duration August 13, 2022 at 4:30 AM through October 13, 2022 (p.10, p.126, p.19).
* Volume: volume to fill 120-inch main between LHWTP and IMC approximately 81 million gallons (p.18).
* Impact: By Sunday August 14 at 11:30 a.m., BWAs lifted for all but seven communities representing approximately 133,000 people (p.13); BWAs lifted for remaining seven by Saturday August 20 (p.14).
* Emergency feeds: GCDC estimated providing 2-5 MGD in addition to supplying City of Flint average demand 14 MGD (p.13); Cities of Lapeer and Imlay City, Village of Almont each began use independent emergency connection on August 13 (p.13); GLWA requested City of Flint close FL-01 valve August 25 to provide water to Lapeer, Mayfield, Imlay City (p.18); DTE Greenwood could not be served while 120-inch out (p.18).
* Post-break assessment: Tools included electromagnetic survey to locate wire breaks, and visual/sounding inspections to determine if evidence of imminent failure (p.15); Xylem/Pure team inspected 4.4 miles August 22-25 (p.15).
  * PICA inspected 0.5 miles east of break and 1.0 miles west of break (p.14); Total Inspected Length: 8,321.13 ft (1.58 miles) (p.126); Number Analyzed Pipes: 525 (p.126); inspected approximately 1.58 miles on August 23-24 (p.15).
  * Pure conducted east of failure August 22, 2022 (p.141-p.142); inspected section spanned 0.62 miles (p.144); Pure inspected 0.62 miles east of break (p.14).
  * Pure west August 23 to August 25, 2022 (p.168); inspected section spanned 3.77 miles (p.172); sections from Station 14+84 to 23+47 and 65+31 to 68+19 not inspected due high water levels (p.172).
* HDR cause study: GLWA tasked HDR to study failure to determine probable cause(s) under LSIP. Study reviewed operational records, field investigations, and laboratory testing of failed pipe (p.17).
* Definitions (per collection):
  * PCCP: Prestressed concrete cylinder pipe with steel cylinder, concrete and prestress wire wrapped outside to withstand design pressure; embedded type in this main (p.7, p.126).
  * Wire-break (WB) region: Electromagnetic wire-break detection: PICA Near-Field Testing (NFT) and Pure PipeWalker; regions reported as estimated number of broken prestress wire wraps; uncalibrated estimates where no pipe-specific calibration performed (p.15, p.146, p.174).
  * Firm capacity: Capacity with largest unit of station out of service — applied to LHWTP/IMC pumping and to transmission redundancy (sole conduit vs looped/emergency-feed capability) (p.9).

## Design Criteria

1. Normal operating / design pressure limit for 120-inch main — up to 200 psi normal operating; 200 psi design - operating pressure did not exceed design immediately leading up to event (p.7 and p.17).
2. Water emergency and EGLE reporting basis — loss of pressure / lack of adequate supply = water emergency requiring discovery, cause, corrective actions, public notice, affected area, duration and coping ability report (p.16, Rule R325.10104(m), Rule R325.12304).
3. Repair disinfection and return-to-service test — fill/chlorinate/flush, chlorine residuals to normal, pressure testing, 3 consecutive days bacteriological samples passing before BWA lift and restoration to service (p.18-p.19, R325.11109-10; AWWA C651-14 slug method; PA 399).
4. Post-break electromagnetic condition assessment extent — dewatered main inspected: PICA NFT ~1.58 miles at break (0.5 mi east + 1.0 mi west); Pure PipeWalker 0.62 mi east + 3.77 mi west; visual/sounding for imminent failure; wire-break regions quantified (p.14-p.15, p.126, p.144, p.172).
5. PCCP distress screening for this study — inventory distress: PICA 7 WB regions max 15 WBs, 0 regions >15 WBs; Pure West 27/1248 pipes with broken wraps; use max count, spatial clustering and visual distress to judge immediate replacement need - no >15-WB region found in inspected length (p.126, p.134, p.169). **Note: superseded by QA/QC — Pure West shows 3 pipes >15 up to 25 wraps, see Capacity Evaluation and QA/QC.**
* Standards applied: Michigan Safe Drinking Water Act PA 399; Admin Code R325.10104(m), R325.12304, R325.11109-10; AWWA C651-14 slug method; AWWA Research Foundation / USEPA Romer et al. 2008 Failure of PCCP Table 5.1 benchmark cited p.16-p.17.
