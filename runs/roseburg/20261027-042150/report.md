# Technical Memorandum – Winchester Water Treatment Plant Expansion 12 mgd to 18 mgd
**Facility:** Winchester Water Treatment Plant, Roseburg, Oregon
**Source:** City of Roseburg, Water Treatment Facilities Preliminary Design Report, July 2009 (public record)
**Question:** Can the Winchester WTP be expanded from 12 mgd to 18 mgd? For each unit along the flow path, does it limit 18 mgd, at what flow does it become limiting, and what least-cost modification makes 18 mgd work?

## 1. Purpose
Determine, by unit along the flow path, whether 18 mgd can be achieved from the current 12 mgd rating, the flow at which each unit becomes limiting, and the least-cost modification that makes 18 mgd work, using only the record pages p.107–p.119 and logged hydraulic runs.

## 2. Background
- Current rated plant capacity of 12 mgd. This is a treatment capacity limitation determined by the four existing filters (p.107) [GEN-01].
- Designed to be expanded to a nominal capacity of 18 mgd. This is also a treatment capacity limitation based on an ultimate build out of six filters (p.107) [GEN-02].
- Hydraulic capacity is piping, pumping, volume and flow control limits; treatment capacity is ability to meet regulatory or industry standards (p.107).
- Hydraulic profile design basis: Developed during original design for maximum instantaneous flow of 18 mgd; updated based on field tests for maximum flow of 22 mgd (p.108).
- Recent plant flow rate has ranged from approximately 3.5 mgd during low demand periods to 11.6 mgd during a recent peak summer day (p.108) [GEN-08].
- Water rights on the North Umpqua River totaling 20 mgd (p.107) [GEN-03], which exceeds 18 mgd (p.107).
- Maximum raw water pumping rate with all raw water pumps operating is nominally 22 mgd (p.107) [GEN-04]; during a test on June 24, 2003, the total combined flow of all four raw water pumps was 20.9 mgd (p.107) [GEN-05].
- Maximum instantaneous flow rate of finished water from the high service pump station is nominally 16 mgd with all finished water pumps operating (p.107) [GEN-06]; maximum actually achieved is about 14 mgd (p.107) [GEN-07].

## 3. Design Criteria
- Firm pumping capacity definition: Maximum flow achievable with one of the largest installed pumps out of service (p.108).
- Firm capacity convention: Use tested firm (largest unit out) as governing, not nameplate, for intake, HS, WW stations (p.108, p.115, p.119).
- Maximum desirable pipeline velocity: Typically 6.0 fps in transmission main of this type; short segment of finished main allows higher as acceptable (p.110, p.116).
- Fish protection approach velocity: Maximum approach velocity 0.4 fps applies at 4-ft square gated openings outside fish screens, gates fully submerged (p.109).
- Clearwell storage guideline: Generally desirable minimum ~60 minutes detention at peak flow rate (p.115).
- Surge protection performance criteria: Protection to 9700 gpm (14.0 mgd); max upsurge 150 psi; min downsurge at HS station HGL 630 ft (~175 ft head or 75 psi assuming ground 455 ft) (p.117).
- Filter treatment rating basis: 3 mgd per filter treatment (12 mgd /4 filters; 18 mgd /6 filters same media); hydraulically 4 mgd per filter filtering to clearwell (p.107, p.112).

## 9. Recommendations
Expansion 12 mgd (p.107) to 18 mgd (p.107) is feasible only with all of the following least-cost package:

1. INT-FISH intake fish-compliance improvement per Sec 6 options (p.109) – binds at ~16.5 mgd.
2. RW-A raw refurbish/replace to higher head + VFDs – restores firm from 14 mgd tested (p.109) to 18.462 mgd firm (RW-FIRM-UPGRADE-FIX output).
3. PRE-A second parallel floc/sed basin + second weir (p.111) – raises treatment 12 mgd (p.111) to 18 mgd (p.107/p.111); hydraulics to 22–24 mgd (p.111–p.112).
