# Technical Memorandum – Winchester Water Treatment Plant 12 mgd to 18 mgd Expansion Evaluation

## Purpose

Evaluate whether the Winchester Water Treatment Plant, Roseburg, Oregon can be expanded from current 12 mgd to 18 mgd (p.115) (p.111), and for each unit along the flow path determine whether it limits 18 mgd, the flow at which it becomes limiting, and the least-cost modification that makes 18 mgd work.

Source: City of Roseburg, Water Treatment Facilities Preliminary Design Report, July 2009 (public record) (p.107-p.119) (dwg p.136).

## Background

- Current plant capacity 12 mgd: p.111 rated at 12 mgd; p.114 at current maximum plant flow of 12 mgd; p.115 at the 12 mgd plant capacity.
- Study expansion target 18 mgd: p.115 At the expanded plant capacity of 18 mgd; p.111 At 18 mgd pipeline velocity; p.116 exceed 6.0 fps at approximately 18 mgd.
- Ultimate maximum plant capacity cited in text 24 mgd: p.111 adequate for an ultimate maximum plant capacity of 24 mgd; p.114 accommodate an ultimate plant flow of 24 mgd; p.115 At 24 mgd using the 1-hour criteria.
- Drawing ultimate design flow conflict: dwg p.136 BASED ON AN ULTIMATE PLANT FLOW OF 22 MGD vs text 24 mgd (p.111) (p.114). See QA/QC.
- Flow path for capacity study (p.107-108 general + existing hydraulic profile Figure 4-2): river intake -> raw-water pumps -> 30-in raw-water pipeline -> rapid mix -> flocculation/sedimentation basin (launders, settled-water channel) -> 42-in settled-water pipe -> filter influent channel -> filters (4 existing) -> filter effluent pipes -> clearwell/treated storage (3 zones) -> high-service pumps -> 30-in site transmission main -> offsite transmission to Reservoir Hill reservoirs -> backwash/wastewater/solids side stream.
- Full units_on_path evaluated: RIVER INTAKE; RAW-WATER PUMP STATION; 30-IN RAW-WATER PIPELINE; RAPID MIX; FLOCCULATION BASIN; SEDIMENTATION BASIN LAUNDERS; SETTLED-WATER CHANNEL + OVERFLOW WEIR; 42-IN SETTLED-WATER PIPELINE; FILTER INFLUENT CHANNEL; FILTERS 1-4 (BAYS, MEDIA, UNDERDRAINS); FILTER EFFLUENT PIPING + METERS + CONTROL VALVES; FILTER-TO-WASTE PIPING; CLEARWELL / TREATED STORAGE (3 ZONES); CLEARWELL OVERFLOWS; HIGH-SERVICE PUMP STATION; 30-IN SITE FINISHED-WATER MAIN + HEADER; OFFSITE TRANSMISSION TO RESERVOIR HILL + RESERVOIRS; SURGE TANK; BACKWASH PUMPS + PIPING + AIR SCOUR; BACKWASH DRAINS + CONVEYANCE; WASTEWATER PUMP STATION; WASHWATER/SOLIDS BASINS + DRYING BEDS + DIVERSION MH.
- Observed operation: plant flow rate has ranged from approximately 3.5 mgd during low demand periods to 11.6 mgd during a recent peak summer day (p.108) (DR-01).

## Design Criteria

From collection criteria:

- Firm capacity definition: capacity with largest unit out of service (Engineering rules provided for this study).
- Hydraulic vs treatment capacity definition: p.107 hydraulic capacity is related to piping, pumping, volume and flow control; treatment capacity is related to ability to meet regulatory requirements – evaluate both; treatment can limit below hydraulic.
- Existing pretreatment basin rating: p.111 The single existing basin is rated at 12 mgd.
- Existing filter treatment rating: p.112 current 3 mgd filter rating is a treatment capacity limitation – 3 mgd per filter.
- Filter hydraulic pass-through when filtering to clearwell: p.112 Hydraulically, the piping into and out of each basin can handle 4 mgd when the filter is filtering to the clearwell – 4 mgd per filter hydraulic.
- Observed filter-to-waste piping limit: p.113 existing filter-to-waste piping does not appear to convey more than 2 mgd; p.113 flow through filter No.1 in filter-to-waste was approximately 2 mgd – 2 mgd.
- Clearwell detention desirability criterion: p.115 generally considered desirable that a WTP maintain a minimum clearwell volume of approximately 60 minutes of detention time at the plant's peak flow rate – 60 minutes at peak flow.
- Maximum desirable transmission main velocity: p.116 While the maximum desirable velocity in a main of this type is approximately 6.0 fps – 6.0 fps.
- Surge system performance criteria: p.117 Surge protection is provided for pumping rates up to 9,700 gpm (14.0 mgd); p.117 maximum upsurge controlled to 150 psi; p.117 minimum allowable downsurge controlled to an HGL of 630 feet (approximately 175 feet of head or 75 psi assuming plant ground elevation of 455 feet) – 14.0 mgd, 150 psi max upsurge, HGL 630 ft min downsurge.
- Sedimentation overflow weir rating: p.111 weir is approximately 9 feet long with a capacity of approximately 12 mgd, assuming a 9-inch water depth over the weir – 12 mgd at 9-in depth, 9 ft long, elev 465.0 ft.
- Sedimentation overflow drain pipe rating: p.111 drain pipe is 30-inches in diameter, has a capacity of approximately 10 mgd – 10 mgd, 30-in diameter.
- Clearwell HSPS overflow rating: p.114 At 1 foot of water depth over the weir, the overflow capacity is approximately 19 mgd; p.114 overflow weir and piping is sufficient to handle clearwell overflows up to 19 mgd – 19 mgd at 1-ft depth, weir elev 449.5 ft, length ~9 ft.
- Combined clearwell overflows ultimate rating: p.114 Combined, the two overflow weirs can accommodate an ultimate plant flow of 24 mgd – 24 mgd combined.
- Filter operating level and driving head: p.112 normal filter operating level ranges from 463 to 464 feet; p.112 provides 5 to 6 feet of submergence over top of media; p.112 provides total filter driving head of approximately 15 to 17.5 feet; p.112 alarm signifying terminal filter headloss is currently set at 11.0 feet – 463-464 ft, 5-6 ft submergence, 15-17.5 ft driving head, 11.0 ft terminal alarm.
- Clearwell normal operating band: p.112 normal maximum water level in treated water storage area and clearwell is 449.0 feet, six-inches below elevation of an overflow weir; p.112 normally operated between about 446.5 feet and 448 feet; p.114 maintain clearwell level between approximately 8 feet and 10 feet depth, which is 3 to 1 feet below overflow weir – max 449.0 ft, normal 446.5-448 ft, 8-10 ft depth.
