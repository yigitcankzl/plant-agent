# Technical Memorandum - SWWRF Wet Weather and Liquid Process Capacity Assessment

## Purpose

Evaluate hydraulic capacity today (flow at first top-of-wall overtopping), identify limiting structure, identify which hydraulic elements fail at the 69 MGD peak wet-weather flow after Albert Whitted WRF (AWWRF) diversion, and identify modifications or flow management to handle 69 MGD.

Facility: Southwest Water Reclamation Facility (SWWRF), St. Petersburg, Florida (p.10 Sec 2.1). Source document: Brown and Caldwell for the City of St. Petersburg, SWWRF Wet Weather and Liquid Process Capacity Assessment, Final Report, July 2014 (public record).

## Design Criteria

- Permitted treatment capacity (AADF): 20 MGD annual average daily flow (p.8 Sec 1; p.10 Sec 2.1).
- Study target - future peak wet weather (peak hour) influent to SWWRF after AWWRF diversion: 69.0 MGD Peak Hour influent (52.6 MGD Max Day, 31.4 MGD Max Month, 20.0 MGD Annual Average) (p.22 Table 3-3; p.29 Sec 5.2).
- Basis of 69 MGD projection: 25.5 MGD max dry weather flow + 43.2 MGD max storm contribution = 68.7 MGD rounded to 69.0 MGD peak hour (p.20 Sec 3.1).
- Projected dry-weather diurnal range at 20 MGD design: 14 to 25.5 MGD (diurnal factors 0.71 to 1.27) (p.19 Sec 3.1).
- Observed max storm increment above dry weather 2012-2013: approximately 43.2 MGD (p.20 Sec 3.1).
- Projected total (dry + storm) before rounding: 68.7 = 25.5 + 43.2 MGD (p.20 Sec 3.1).
- Wet-weather loads at design flows (p.22 Table 3-3):
  - CBOD5 lb/d: 35862 / 46621 / 71003 / 89954 (AA/MM/MD/PH).
  - TSS lb/d: 40032 / 52042 / 79259 / 100413.
  - TKN lb/d: 6750 / 8775 / 13364 / 16931.
  - TP lb/d: 998 / 1298 / 1976 / 2504.
- Wet-weather loading assumption for process capacity: pollutant loadings during wet weather similar to dry weather loadings (dilution, concentrations drop below 80 mg/L CBOD5/TSS at peak) (p.21 Sec 3.3).
- Hydraulic capacity definition: flow at which wastewater would overtopple the top of wall for that tank (p.29 Sec 5.2).
- Hydraulic profile basis - modelled flows shown on Figure 5-1: water surfaces modelled at 20 and 40 MGD after Biosolids to Energy improvements; 40 MGD not hydraulically challenged (p.29 Sec 5.2; dwg p.30 title).
- Reported hydraulic capacity and limiting unit: 40.5 MGD limited by the filters (p.29 Sec 5.2).
- Reported 40 MGD check: At 40 MGD flow, none of the unit processes were hydraulically challenged as shown in Figure 5-1 (p.29 Sec 5.2).
- Reported 69 MGD failure description: upon running the future peak wet weather flow of 69 MGD, the hydraulic elements failed to hold proper hydraulic gradients. The losses through the hydraulic elements were so great that the hydraulic grade line in all of the elements exceeded the top of the wall for the simulated process units (p.29 Sec 5.2).
