# Salt MVR synthetic historian: basis and limitations

`historian.csv` is **synthetic**, generated by `tools/make_salt_historian.py` (fixed seed),
not an export from the patented plant. It has 1,440 hourly rows, 2026-08-30 through
2026-10-28. All temperatures are °C, pressures bar absolute, and flows use the units
shown in the column headers. `UI-201` and `DI-201` are calculated scenario signals,
not physical transmitters shown on the P&ID.

## What is grounded in sources

- The [Texas Brine patent, Table I](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5366514A/en)
  supplies the baseline feed (362.9 t/h), V-201 pressure (1.70 bar(a)), V-201 brine
  temperature (125.6 °C), K-201 suction vapour (226.8 t/h), K-201 discharge
  (3.08 bar(a)), purge liquor (47.6 t/h), and dry salt (94.9 t/h), as already converted
  in [basis.md](basis.md). Its compressor passes a fixed amount of vapour and the
  excess heats the purge evaporator. It does **not** give a compressor power rating,
  fouling time series, or the invented instrument readings here.
- The [GE/HPD operating paper in the US EPA Merrimack record, AR-997, pp. 4–7](https://19january2017snapshot.epa.gov/www3/region1/npdes/merrimackstation/pdfs/ar/AR997.pdf)
  reports that mixed-salt crystallizer heater U can fall to about 50% of its clean
  value; water boil-outs are typically needed every 2–6 weeks and take about a
  shift. It says the compressor must be sized for fouled U and brine boiling-point
  rise (BPE). In its California MVR example, clean/dilute throughput was about
  20 gpm, falling to the 15 gpm design rate as concentration and fouling grew;
  soluble-impurity accumulation increased BPE and required purge. Those are
  **analogues**, not observations from the single-salt Texas Brine plant. See also
  the local [research notes, §5](../../../case-data/raw/nacl_mvr/numbers.md).
- [Dash et al., *Langmuir* 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28985072/)
  experimentally show crystallization deposits reducing boiling heat transfer.
  This supports the direction of the U signal, not the arbitrary 60-day rate.

## Scenario construction

The patented point is the reference, with small reproducible measurement noise.
The illustrative `basis.md` gives E-201 U = 2,000 W/m²K in service and 2,500 clean;
this scenario starts near clean (2,450), declines to about 1,600 by day 35, stops
for an eight-hour water boil-out, recovers to 2,450, then declines to about 1,700
by day 60. `UI-201` is this inferred U plus noise. The health check detects the
**post-boil-out** segment rather than fitting one line through the cleaning jump.

From days 18–31, FI-5001 purge is deliberately 20% below its 47.6 t/h setpoint.
An illustrative retained-impurity state rises, then slowly clears when purge is
restored. It adds to a 10.4 K reference BPE, inferred from the patent's 125.6 °C
brine versus 115.2 °C vapour at 1.70 bar(a); TI-201 follows that BPE at nearly
constant PI-201 pressure. This is a **proxy**, not an electrolyte calculation or a
prediction of a real purge setpoint.

To hold boil-up while U falls, the scenario raises required K-201 discharge
pressure. PI-3002 and illustrative 9 MW shaft power rise while FI-3001 remains
near 226.8 t/h. FI-3000 totals the patent's 226.8 t/h compressor suction plus
20.9 t/h excess vapour to E-301. At an **invented** 3.20 bar(a) discharge ceiling,
pressure stops rising and vapour/production derate. Cleaning restores the lower pressure and
full production. The power and 3.20 bar(a) ceiling are not patent ratings.

The brine rise TI-2003 minus TI-2002 grows because the scenario also reduces
P-201 circulation as fouling develops. **U loss alone cannot increase that rise
at constant heat duty and mass flow**: `Q = m·cp·ΔT`. The measured rise remains
below the 3 K screen in `basis.md`. The `DI-201` column makes the screen visible
to the morning check.

## Limits of the synthetic data

The illustrative P-201 rating in `basis.md` (15,000 m³/h), patent vapour boil-up
(roughly 227 t/h through K-201), and a ≤3 K brine heater rise do not close an
energy balance at ordinary brine density and heat capacity: 15,000 m³/h ×
1,200 kg/m³ × 3.3 kJ/(kg·K) × 3 K / 3,600 ≈ 50 MW, while condensing the
patent vapour is on the order of 140 MW. We preserve the stated baseline and
label this as an illustrative diagnostic scenario, **not a calibrated heat/mass
balance or a real historian**. A real plant would need actual circulation rates,
steam duties, compressor curves, brine chemistry, and lab BPE before using these
signals for operations or a capacity decision.

The generic `historian` tool and model-independent morning check can read this
case now. The existing DWSIM `troubleshoot` fitter assumes a conventional
process-hot/utility-cold exchanger and a built model with matching stream IDs;
E-201 is a steam-heated brine evaporator and this case has no committed model.
Accordingly its direct-U signal is **not advertised as DWSIM-troubleshootable**.
Supporting a physical E-201 diagnosis would require changing that fitter and
calibrating a model, neither of which this data-generation task does.
