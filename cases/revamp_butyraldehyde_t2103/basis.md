# T-2103 butyraldehyde purification tower: blind operating basis

Source: Summers, Monaco and Britto, AIChE Spring Meeting paper 112b (2013), pp. 1-5. This sheet stops before the later corrective actions and performance tests.

## Existing tower

- T-2103 separates iso-butyraldehyde overhead from normal butyraldehyde bottoms in the South American Oxo-alcohol plant (paper, Background).
- Shell diameter: **1,900 mm**. The tower has **90 trays** with **400-450 mm** spacing (paper, Background).
- Existing internals at the start of the study: normal straight-downcomer RV-1 round-valve trays. Downcomers occupy **6.2%** of tower cross-sectional area (paper, Background).
- The observed overall tray efficiency was about **71%**, and stable feed was limited near **9.5 metric t/h** (paper, Background).
- There are two thermosyphon reboilers. The measured heat input during the capacity test was about **4.1 million kcal/h**, roughly **25% below design** (paper, capacity test discussion).

## Pre-solution test evidence

- Feed composition was not published. For DWSIM, use iso- and normal-butyraldehyde with UNIQUAC/NRTL and label the feed split as an assumption.
- Steam to the two reboilers was raised from **6.9 to 7.3 t/h** while reflux was held constant (paper, capacity test discussion).
- Across that change, total tower pressure drop rose by **less than 1%**. Above about **7.2 t/h** steam, reflux-drum level and the temperature profile became difficult to control (paper, capacity test discussion).
- Raising reflux did not drive the temperature profile back down the tower. The response was unlike a progressive liquid backup indicated by a conventional hydraulic flood (paper, capacity test discussion).
- Flowmeters for reflux, feed and steam were checked during the investigation (paper, capacity test discussion).

## Project premise

The intended feed rate is **11.5 t/h**, about **20% above** the established 9.5 t/h limit. Determine the physical mechanism, then find a design and operating point that reaches that feed while retaining the two isomer products. Do not treat an assumed feed assay or assumed tray rating as plant evidence.
