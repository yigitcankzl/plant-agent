# NZRC Whangarei CDU-1 main fractionator C-150: blind operating basis

Source: a refining-journal field report. The citation is withheld from the agent because it identifies the outcome; it is recorded with the withheld outcome in `outcome.json` and in the case's `source` field.

## Existing process

- CDU-1 processes **8,500 t/d** of crude through a desalter, three preheat trains, two furnaces, a preflash vessel and main fractionator C-150 (paper Figure 1 and project premise).
- C-150 produces unstabilised naphtha, kerosene, light gas oil, heavy gas oil and long residue. Kerosene, LGO and HGO use stripping steam (paper Figure 1).
- The heavy-gas-oil pumparound section contains **three contacting trays** at **500 mm** spacing. Before the project these were first-generation Shell Calming Section trays (paper, C-150 existing internals description).
- Other existing sections use conventional or first-generation high-capacity trays and a wash section. The paper does not publish a machine-readable complete crude assay or all section dimensions.

## Modelling boundary

- Model C-150 with petroleum pseudo-components or documented boiling-range stand-ins. State every assumed assay fraction and relative-volatility choice.
- Keep the existing shell and the three-tray HGO pumparound geometry fixed in the base model.
- The source reports **13,000 t/d** as the project intake. Product-cut quality has to remain comparable to the existing crude unit.
- A crude-assay or absolute shell-diameter assumption is a scale assumption, not published plant data.

## Project premise

Raise crude intake from **8,500 to 13,000 t/d** (**1.5294x**) in the existing main fractionator. Identify the controlling section and the smallest physical scope that retains the five product cuts. Check the preheat train and furnace as adjacent constraints rather than assuming the tower is the only limit.
