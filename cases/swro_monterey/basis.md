# Monterey Peninsula desalination plant: data sheet

Numbers are from the California American Water request for proposals for design and construction of desalination infrastructure, issued 17 June 2013, a 642-page PDF (RFP PDF p.1). Citations are PDF page numbers. Printed page numbers restart in each section; both are given where a printed number is on the page. Anything the RFP does not state is marked (ILLUSTRATIVE).

The cost workshop downloaded with the RFP does not fix intake-well count or RO vessel count, and no figure below is taken from it.

## Capacity

Rated capacity is the flow that can be treated and delivered with the single largest pump or RO train out of service (RFP PDF p.20, printed 2-10; RFP PDF p.332, printed 2-5). The rated capacity is 9.6 MGD, or 6.4 MGD if the 3,500 acre-foot-per-year groundwater replenishment project proceeds (RFP PDF p.332). Annual delivery at those rates is 10,752 acre-feet per year at 9.6 MGD and 7,168 acre-feet per year at 6.4 MGD (RFP PDF p.331, printed 2-4).

An ultimate capacity of 12.8 MGD is the basis for fixed facilities and for space planning. It is the 9.6 MGD rated plant plus two RO trains of 1.6 MGD each, or the 6.4 MGD rated plant plus four RO trains of 1.6 MGD each (RFP PDF p.332). Maximum capacity means the spare RO train is also running. Pretreatment, chemical feed and disinfection must be able to reach maximum capacity with no extra redundancy (RFP PDF p.333, printed 2-6). The first-pass RO minimum daily production is 3.2 MGD (RFP PDF p.343, printed 2-16).

Derived from those sentences, not printed as a train count: 9.6 / 1.6 = 6 trains online, plus one spare, is 7 trains at the 9.6 MGD rating; 12.8 / 1.6 = 8 trains online, plus one spare, is 9 trains at the ultimate rating (derived from RFP PDF p.332). Separately, the maximum size of one train is 2 MGD of permeate (RFP PDF p.346, printed 2-19).

## Units, intake to product

Slant beach wells and the well pumps are a separate procurement. For this proposal the source hydraulic grade line at the plant is 155 feet MSL. Well type, number and location are not decided. Power for the wells is not taken from the plant (RFP PDF p.337, printed 2-10). Well count and intake-pump curves are therefore not available. ILLUSTRATIVE: do not invent a well count.

Raw water is oxidized with sodium hypochlorite, then granular media filtration, dechlorination, sulfuric acid, cartridge filtration, first-pass seawater RO, a partial second-pass brackish RO, ultraviolet light, carbon dioxide with either calcite or hydrated lime, sodium hydroxide, orthophosphate, and post-chlorination (RFP PDF p.331).

Sodium hypochlorite is generated on site. Three generators, 500 pounds per day each, may run together. Raw-water dose 0.5 / 2 / 3 mg/L (min / avg / max). Finished-water dose 1 / 1.5 / 2 mg/L. Two salt dissolvers, 23 tons usable each. Two storage tanks, about 6,500 gallons each (RFP PDF p.376 and p.377). An in-line static mixer disperses the oxidant ahead of the filters. A mixer bypass is not required (RFP PDF p.338, printed 2-11).

Media filters are dual media: 12 inches of sand (0.45 to 0.55 mm) and 18 inches of anthracite (0.65 to 0.85 mm), AWWA B100, steel vessels with 1/4 inch neoprene, ASME stamped (RFP PDF p.338). Filtration rate shall not exceed 4 gpm/sf with one filter washing and a second filter out for maintenance. Allow 5 percent extra flow for washwater recycle (RFP PDF p.338). The number of filters and the total area are not stated. ILLUSTRATIVE: a filter count or an area computed from 4 gpm/sf is not an RFP figure. Backwash comes from the filtered-water tanks, two pumps (one duty, one reserve), one filter at a time, 30 percent bed expansion at the highest temperature, unit waste volume 200 gal/sf/wash (RFP PDF p.340, printed 2-13). Two washwater basins, batch fill-settle-draw. Recycle pumps: two duty and one reserve, with VFDs. Recycle stays under 10 percent of influent and under 2 NTU (RFP PDF p.341, printed 2-14).

Filtered-water tanks: two, 300,000 gallons each, glass-lined bolted steel, aluminum dome. The plant runs at rated capacity with one tank out (RFP PDF p.341). Filtered-water pumps lift through the cartridge filters into the RO. Two pumps at 50 percent (one duty, one reserve) and two at 25 percent (both duty); VFDs on the two smaller pumps; super duplex; outdoors (RFP PDF p.342, printed 2-15). No head or motor size is stated.

Sodium bisulfite, 38 percent, specific gravity 1.33, quenches chlorine at the filter discharge and at the concentrate discharge. The dose is marked TBD. One bulk tank, 6,000 gallons. An ORP sensor confirms dechlorination. Two metering pumps per application point (RFP PDF p.377 and p.378).

Sulfuric acid, 50 percent, specific gravity 1.4, is dosed to filtered water at 5 / 10 / 30 mg/L (min / avg / max). One bulk tank, recommended 10,000 gallons (RFP PDF p.379, printed 2-52).

Threshold inhibitor (antiscalant), specific gravity 1.25, is dosed at the first-pass inlet and the second-pass inlet at 2 / 3 / 6 mg/L. One bulk tank, 6,300 gallons, with a weighed day tank (RFP PDF p.379 and p.380).

Cartridge filters: one vessel per RO train, 5 micron nominal, polypropylene string-wound, 40 inch, at most 4 gpm per 10 inch length. Clean differential pressure at most 4 psi; changeout at 20 psi; at least two months between changes. Vessel design pressure at least 150 psi, AL-6XN, horizontal (RFP PDF p.345, printed 2-18). SDI and turbidity limits are above.

First-pass high-pressure feed pumps: centrifugal, one per train, each with a VFD, minimum efficiency 80 percent, super duplex. They must cover the full temperature, salinity, fouling and energy-recovery range (RFP PDF p.348, printed 2-21).

Energy recovery: one positive-displacement device per train, on the first-pass concentrate, minimum efficiency 95 percent, mixing at most 3 percent when the low-pressure and high-pressure flows match and recovery is under 50 percent, life at least 20 years. A booster pump per device: horizontal multistage, wetted parts AL-6XN or titanium, product-lubricated (RFP PDF p.350, printed 2-23). The RFP does not name a manufacturer or the product name PX. ILLUSTRATIVE: calling the device a PX is a class inference, not a specification.

First-pass RO (RFP PDF p.346 and p.347):

- Recovery at least 42 percent and at most 45 percent (RFP PDF p.346).
- Maximum flux 8.75 gfd (RFP PDF p.346).
- Maximum feed pressure 1,000 psi (RFP PDF p.346).
- At most 7 elements per vessel. Vessels stacked at most 6 high (RFP PDF p.346).
- Elements are 8 inch, 400 square feet, seawater RO, at least 99.6 percent salt rejection and 90 percent boron rejection (RFP PDF p.347).
- Frames must hold 10 percent more vessels than the design needs (RFP PDF p.346).
- Pressure vessels are rated at least 1,200 psig (RFP PDF p.348).
- There is no recycle of second-pass concentrate to the head of the plant as a base rule (RFP PDF p.346), and a recycle line is also required for flexibility (RFP PDF p.347). Both sentences are in the RFP.

The number of vessels and elements is not stated. (ILLUSTRATIVE) screening at the flux cap, not a design: 1.6 MGD is 1,600,000 gpd; divided by 8.75 gfd that is 182,857 square feet; divided by 400 square feet that is 458 elements; at 7 per vessel that is 66 vessels. A real design will use a lower flux and a different count.

The RFP gives one maximum membrane flux rate for the first-pass train; it does not give a separate lead-element flux
limit (RFP PDF p.346). The WaterTAP screen therefore compares 8.75 gfd with train-average permeate flow divided by
installed membrane area. Feed-end flux is still reported as diagnostic output, but is not checked against an invented
second limit.

Second pass: at least 40 percent of first-pass permeate, or a complete second pass (RFP PDF p.343). Maximum recovery 90 percent, maximum flux 18 gfd, at most 7 elements per vessel, brackish elements at least 99.0 percent rejection, feed pH at most 10. Caustic is dosed to raise pH for boron removal (RFP PDF p.347). One VFD feed pump per second-pass train, minimum efficiency 70 percent (RFP PDF p.349, printed 2-22). Sodium hydroxide, 50 percent, specific gravity 1.4: second-pass dose 5 / 10 / 20 mg/L and finished-water dose 2 / 3 / 5 mg/L (RFP PDF p.381, printed 2-54).

UV is downstream of the membranes and upstream of stabilization. Case 1 is the basis of design: 2-log Cryptosporidium from UV on top of 2-log from RO, toward 4-log Cryptosporidium, 5-log Giardia and 6-log virus. Reactors are N+1, previously validated, minimum UVT 95 percent, maximum flow at least 11.2 MGD for the 9.6 MGD design. A required alternative deletes UV if the regulator agrees (RFP PDF p.364 and p.365).

Post-stabilization must hold hardness and alkalinity from 40 to 100 mg/L as CaCO3 at an LSI of 0 to +0.2, and must also be able to run at pH 7.7 for phosphoric acid (RFP PDF p.331). Calcite contactors (option 2): empty-bed contact time at least 20 minutes, at least one standby, carbon dioxide before the contactors, carbon dioxide and caustic after them. Carbon dioxide storage is at least 30 days, one tank, two vaporizers, two vapor heaters (RFP PDF p.362 and p.363). The hydrated-lime alternative is also a required price: at 9.6 MGD the limewater flow is 114 to 286 gpm and saturated limewater is 1.8 g/L as Ca(OH)2 at 12 C (RFP PDF p.361, printed 2-34).

Zinc orthophosphate, PO4:Zn of 5:1, dose as PO4 0.5 / 1.0 / 4.0 mg/L (min / avg / max), with a future conversion to phosphoric acid (RFP PDF p.382, printed 2-55). The finished-water table still caps the owner set point at 3.5 mg/L as PO4 (RFP PDF p.431).

Finished-water storage: two tanks in series, 750,000 gallons each. The 1-log Giardia CT volume at 9.6 MGD, 1.2 mg/L free chlorine, pH 8.0, baffling 0.5 and 10 C is 615,000 gallons (RFP PDF p.366, printed 2-39). With one tank out, residual, flow or level band may have to change.

Finished-water pumps: four pumps, firm capacity 9.6 MGD with the largest out. Two at 50 percent of rated capacity, constant speed with soft starts; two at 25 percent with VFDs. Distribution grade line at the site is given as a preliminary maximum of 425 feet. Pumping head from a storage elevation of 120 feet is 305 feet plus plant losses (RFP PDF p.368, printed 2-41). One 25,000 gallon hydropneumatic tank is included for the proposal's transient study (RFP PDF p.369, printed 2-42).

A separate return to the Salinas Valley sends finished water to the Castroville pond: two pumps, 1.2 MGD each, head expected at or below 30 feet, with VFDs, through a 1.2 mile, 12 inch pipeline that is outside this contract beyond the site boundary (RFP PDF p.367, printed 2-40).

## Brine

First-pass and second-pass concentrate go to the MRWPCA outfall. Piping beyond the site boundary is by others. The effluent junction structure is at 101.0 feet MSL and is not pressurized (RFP PDF p.358, printed 2-31). On-site equalization lagoon: at least 3,000,000 gallons, minimum freeboard 3 feet, emptied by pumps over 12 hours (one or two duty plus a reserve) (RFP PDF p.358 and p.359). Reject handling is designed for no more than 75 percent permeate recovery (RFP PDF p.358). Aeration downstream of the RO, preferably a cascade or tray aerator, is proposed because beach-well water is low in dissolved oxygen; the 2009 Coastal Water Project EIR, as cited in the RFP, recommended 5.0 mg/L dissolved oxygen in the discharge (RFP PDF p.358 and p.359). The NPDES conditions were not identified at the time of the RFP (RFP PDF p.358).
