# Withheld: 2008 pilot outcome

Not a project document. The process workspace reads basis.md, the drawing and the documents listed in case.json. This file is not one of them. Do not give it to the agent. It is here so a later comparison can see what the pilot actually concluded.

Source: NRS Consulting Engineers, Texas Seawater Desalination Demonstration Project, Final Pilot Study Report, October 2008, for the Brownsville Public Utilities Board. PDF page numbers below.

The pilot found seawater desalination on the Brownsville Ship Channel technically feasible. Microfiltration followed by reverse osmosis took the raw seawater to potable standards. The ship channel was still described as a difficult source because turbidity, suspended solids and temperature vary sharply. A full-scale 25 MGD design was considered possible from the pilot data, but it would have to be conservative, and therefore expensive, because red tides and hurricanes were not seen during the pilot (2008 PDF p.7).

The recommendation is not to build the 25 MGD plant next. BPUB proposes a 2.5 MGD demonstration plant and research facility at the Port of Brownsville, with the intake, concentrate disposal and land bought at full-scale size so a later expansion is cheaper. Estimated cost of that demonstration project is 67,479,000 dollars, of which about half is the full-scale intake and disposal (2008 PDF p.7 and p.14). The phased capacities drawn in the executive summary are 2.5 MGD demonstration, then 12.5 MGD, then 25 MGD (2008 PDF p.14).

Both FilmTec and Toray elements met the pilot goals (2008 PDF p.12). Post-treatment that met primary and secondary standards was caustic soda, sodium bicarbonate and calcium chloride, not the 2004 pebble-lime or calcite screening (2008 PDF p.12). For a 25 MGD potable plant the pilot estimated about 30 MGD of concentrate at about twice the raw salinity, and preferred a Gulf diffuser over Class I injection wells on cost (2008 PDF p.12). That concentrate flow is not the 16.67 MGD figure in the 2004 study.
